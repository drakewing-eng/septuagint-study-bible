About this reader

The Greek Septuagint (Rahlfs 1935), with every word tappable: lemma, parsing in plain English, a short English gloss, and links to full lexicon entries. Built for beginners reading the LXX for theology.

How to use

  • Tap/click any Greek word: the panel shows the form, its dictionary form (lemma), parsing, gloss, and links. All other forms of the same lemma in the chapter are highlighted.
  • ☰ opens the book list; ‹ › move by chapter (arrow keys on desktop). Your last chapter is remembered on this device.
  • EN shows/hides Brenton's English; ◐ dark/light; Aa text size; ⌕ search by Greek lemma (accents optional), transliteration or English.
  • Psalms use Greek (LXX) numbering: LXX Ps 9–147 are generally one lower than Hebrew/English numbering (LXX Ps 22 = English Ps 23).

  • CATSS LXX — the computer form prepared by the TLG (Thesaurus Linguae Graecae) Project directed by T. Brunner at the University of California, Irvine, with further verification and adaptation by CATSS.

English: Sir Lancelot C. L. Brenton, The Septuagint Version of the Old Testament (1851), public domain, text from eBible.org. Matched to the Greek by chapter and verse number only; Brenton's base text and versification are not identical to Rahlfs, so some verses (notably in Esther, Proverbs, Sirach, Jeremiah, Kingdoms) may be offset or missing. No English is shown for Odes, Psalms of Solomon, Tobit (S), Joshua (A), Judges (A), and the Old Greek Daniel/Susanna/Bel.

Known limitations

  • The source text has no punctuation or capitalisation (as in the CCAT data).
  • Glosses are short; always check Logeion/LSJ for full meaning. Morphological tagging comes from the CATSS database and may contain errors.
